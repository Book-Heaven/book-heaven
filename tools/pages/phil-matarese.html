Phil Matarese on Faith, Reflection, and Growing Up Pentecostal

Some stories take years to find their voice. For Phil Matarese, Growing Up Pentecostal: A Memoir was one of them.

Rather than rushing to tell his story, Phil waited until he felt mature enough to approach his experiences with honesty, balance, and respect. The result is a personal account of growing up in a Pentecostal environment, questioning familiar beliefs, and discovering how faith can develop as we move through different stages of life.

Published by Resource Publications, Growing Up Pentecostal: A Memoir explores the experiences that shaped Phil's early understanding of Christianity and the spiritual questions that followed him into adulthood.

A childhood unlike what he later came to know

Growing up Pentecostal meant that church services could be unpredictable. As Phil recalls, there was often little formal structure, and spontaneous moments such as testimonies, speaking in tongues, prophecy, and prayer could happen at any time.

His later experiences in more traditional church settings introduced him to a very different approach to worship. That contrast helped him recognize just how distinctive his childhood religious environment had been.

One particularly important turning point came when his high-school friends visited his church. Their reactions made Phil see his familiar surroundings through someone else's eyes. Practices he had once assumed were common to all Christians suddenly seemed unusual.

That experience raised questions that would become part of his journey toward understanding his own faith.

Finding the courage to tell the story

Phil first considered writing the memoir several years before he actually began working on it. At the time, he worried that he might be too emotional to tell the story fairly.

He chose to wait until he could reflect on his experiences with greater maturity.

Writing the book also required careful decisions about privacy. Phil explains that he chose not to include certain stories because they were too personal to the people involved. He also changed names and identifying details where appropriate.

For him, writing a memoir was not simply about recounting the past. It meant revisiting memories, processing emotions, and finding a respectful way to share experiences that had shaped him.

He describes the writing process as challenging, but ultimately therapeutic.

A message of reflection rather than judgment

One of the strongest ideas running through Phil's memoir is the importance of examining what we believe and understanding why we believe it.

He hopes readers will remain open to exploring other faith traditions, even when they feel settled in their own. In his view, meaningful conversations can reveal that people from different religious backgrounds often share more than they initially realize.

Importantly, Phil did not write the book to mock Pentecostalism or dismiss the people who shaped his childhood. He wanted to present his experiences honestly while recognizing that his perspective has continued to grow.

His message is not that readers must abandon their beliefs. Rather, he encourages them to reflect, ask questions, and develop a faith they can understand and genuinely call their own.

Holding on to faith while continuing to grow

Phil's journey did not lead him to walk away from Christianity. Instead, his experiences encouraged him to explore different expressions of the faith and develop a spiritual outlook that reflected his own convictions.

He hopes readers who recognize parts of their own stories in his memoir will understand that questioning aspects of their upbringing does not necessarily mean losing their faith.

Growth can involve reconsidering old assumptions, learning from other traditions, and finding a way forward that feels sincere.

It is this balance between honest reflection and continued faith that gives the memoir its distinctive perspective.

Looking ahead: A new exploration of fatherhood

Phil is also working on another religious memoir, Father?: Attempting to Define Fatherhood.

Inspired in part by the experiences he and his wife had while fostering, the forthcoming book takes a different direction. Phil describes its middle section as incorporating his own developing scholarship on the meaning of fatherhood.

He expects the book to be published by Resource Publications, an imprint of Wipf and Stock, in early 2027.

It represents another opportunity for Phil to explore meaningful questions through personal reflection and writing.

A story that invites readers to think

With Growing Up Pentecostal: A Memoir, Phil Matarese offers more than a recollection of childhood experiences. He invites readers to consider how their upbringing shapes them, how their beliefs evolve, and how they can remain open to learning throughout life.

His story reminds us that personal growth does not always require rejecting where we came from. Sometimes, it means looking back with greater understanding and deciding what we want to carry forward.
